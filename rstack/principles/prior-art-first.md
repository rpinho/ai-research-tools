# Prior art first, and run it

**The rule.** Search what exists before spending anything, and again whenever a direction stops
producing. Then do the part everyone skips: make the published result RUN.

**The evidence.** Three of four candidate reports from one lane had already been published by
other people. The prior art had been found and read; it had never been run against our own
targets. Another lane spent about 38 rounds in one method family after its own notes had already
named a better one.

**Enforced by** prior-art receipts required before promotion, with structured source dispositions
rather than a claim of having looked; and a baseline tool that registers a published number as a
command, refusing to record one that does not reproduce. You cannot write down a baseline you have
not run. A later re-check catches the quieter failure: a baseline rots when a dependency changes,
and the comparison you have been making for a month is against a number nothing can still produce.

A number that comes out better than published still counts as reproducing the method. Refusing
that would be the harness calling your own improvement a failure.
